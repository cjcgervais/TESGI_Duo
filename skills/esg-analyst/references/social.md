# Social Assessment Reference

## Purpose

This reference guides social impact evaluation for land acquisition decisions. Social factors can create legal barriers, community opposition, or ethical concerns that affect project viability and client alignment.

## Assessment Categories

### 1. Heritage and Cultural Significance

#### Indigenous / First Nations Heritage
| Check | Data Source | Risk Indicator |
|-------|-------------|----------------|
| Aboriginal heritage register | State heritage database | Registered sites |
| Native title claims | National Native Title Tribunal | Active claims |
| Indigenous Land Use Agreement | NNTT, state land council | ILUA in place |
| Cultural heritage assessment | Aboriginal heritage survey | Sensitivity mapping |

**Jurisdictional Notes:**
- Australia: Aboriginal Heritage Acts vary by state; due diligence obligations differ
- Requirements may include Cultural Heritage Management Plan (CHMP)
- Native title can coexist with freehold in some circumstances

**Red Flags:**
- Registered sacred site on or adjacent to property
- Active native title claim without ILUA
- Previous heritage destruction on site
- Proximity to known archaeological areas

#### Non-Indigenous Heritage
| Check | Data Source | Risk Indicator |
|-------|-------------|----------------|
| Heritage overlay | Planning certificate | HO listing |
| Heritage register | State/national heritage database | Statutory listing |
| Contributory building | Heritage precinct maps | Demolition controls |
| Archaeological sensitivity | Council heritage advisor | Subsurface potential |

**Red Flags:**
- Heritage-listed building requiring substantial works
- Contributory status in heritage precinct
- Archaeological investigation condition on permits

### 2. Community Impact

#### Neighborhood and Character
| Check | Data Source | Risk Indicator |
|-------|-------------|----------------|
| Neighborhood character study | Council planning documents | Character provisions |
| Design and Development Overlay | Planning certificate | Height/setback controls |
| Community opposition history | Council meeting minutes, media | Previous objections |
| Resident/action groups | Local media, social media | Organized opposition |

#### Infrastructure and Services
| Check | Data Source | Risk Indicator |
|-------|-------------|----------------|
| School catchments | Education department | Capacity constraints |
| Healthcare access | Health service mapping | Distance to services |
| Public transport | Transport authority | Service frequency |
| Road capacity | Council traffic studies | Congestion issues |

**Red Flags:**
- Contentious development history on site or street
- Active community campaign against similar development
- Infrastructure contribution requirements exceed budget
- Essential services at capacity

### 3. Access and Displacement

#### Existing Occupants
| Check | Data Source | Risk Indicator |
|-------|-------------|----------------|
| Current tenants | Lease documents, agent disclosure | Lease terms |
| Residential tenancies law | State tenancy legislation | Notice periods |
| Relocation obligations | Contract conditions | Vendor undertakings |
| Occupant vulnerability | Due diligence inquiries | Hardship factors |

#### Access Rights
| Check | Data Source | Risk Indicator |
|-------|-------------|----------------|
| Public access / right of way | Title search, survey | Registered easements |
| Crown land interface | State lands department | Access dependencies |
| Landlocked status | Survey, title | Legal access route |
| Shared driveways | Title, body corporate | Access agreements |

**Red Flags:**
- Long-term tenants with statutory protections
- Vulnerable occupants (elderly, disabled, low-income)
- No legal road access (landlocked)
- Disputed access arrangements

## Ethical Considerations

TESGI Advisory does not advocate for or against development. When social impacts are significant:

1. **State facts neutrally** — Do not minimize or exaggerate
2. **Preserve client autonomy** — Decision remains theirs
3. **Flag ethical dimensions** — Note when impacts affect vulnerable parties
4. **Avoid advocacy** — Advisory role, not activism

If client objectives conflict with significant social harm:
- Document the tension explicitly
- Ensure client understands implications
- "Walk away" is a valid recommendation
